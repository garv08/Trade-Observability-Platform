TOP user guide

A local research desk for the Nifty 50, all BSE stocks, and NSE/BSE ETFs. It has daily charts, 153 indicators, Scott Carney harmonics, 95 backtestable strategies (including rules adapted from two YouTube channels), a one-click trend scan, and Screener.in fundamentals.

This desk uses delayed public daily data. It is not a live NSE feed, not a broker, and not TradingView or the Harmonic Trader product. Nothing here is investment advice.

Start the desk

cd Trade-Observability-Platform
source .venv/bin/activate   # see README for install
top-platform           # or: python -m top

Open http://127.0.0.1:8000 (or http://localhost:8000; the server answers on both IPv4 and IPv6 loopback).

On the first start, the desk fills data/nse.duckdb (in the folder you start it from, or the path in TOP_DB) with about five years of daily bars for all 50 names. Later starts reuse the stored data.

The screen

Left panel: every control Stock searchable dropdown.
Indicators: one searchable dropdown per section, in this order: Harmonics, Moving averages, Bands and channels, Trend, Momentum, Volatility, Volume, Price statistics. Below them, On the chart lists active studies with their settings.
Backtest: searchable strategy dropdown, its settings, capital, cost, Run backtest, and Backtest universe.
Analysis: Harmonics list, Trend scan, Fundamentals, and Verify.
Right: chartCandles and volume, overlays and harmonic patterns on price, and oscillator panes. Nothing is drawn over the chart. Directly below it is the Interpretation pane, which explains your selections and gives a swing plan. Below that, the output panel shows Harmonics list, Trend scan, Backtest universe, Fundamentals, and Verify results. It scrolls on its own, and Close gives the chart its full height back. Backtest results come last: metric cards, equity curve, and trade list.

The top bar shows the selected stock and the DuckDB coverage pill, for example DuckDB 50/50. It also has Guide and Sync history. Windows narrower than 1000px hide the left panel. Click Controls in the top bar to open it as a drawer.

Using a dropdown

  • Click a box, or its ▾ arrow, to open the full list. Start typing to filter it. Every word you type must match, so financ bank lists only the banks.
  • Use ↑ and ↓ to move, Enter to pick, and Esc to close.
  • The Stock and Strategy dropdowns hold one choice. Picking one closes the list.
  • Indicator dropdowns allow several choices. Picking a study adds it, and picking it again removes it. The list stays open so you can add more. A ✓ marks studies that are on, and the section title shows how many, for example 2 on.

Pick a stock

Universe chips above the Stock box choose what the dropdown and Backtest universe cover: Nifty 50 (Yahoo, adjusted), Indices, ETFs (~355 NSE + ~35 BSE-only), NSE (every NSE main-board stock: the Nifty 50 plus ~2,600 more, no SME), BSE (~4,700 equities, grouped by BSE group), and All. These chips appear after Sync history, which downloads five years of exchange files the first time (1–2 hours). These prices are adjusted for splits and bonus issues. Symbols carry an exchange prefix, such as BSE:ABB, NSE:IRCTC or NSE:NIFTYBEES. Large lists show the first 300 matches; keep typing to narrow them.

  1. Click the Stock box, or press / from anywhere.
  2. Type a symbol, a company name, or a sector, for example infy, mahindra, pharma, or energy. The list is grouped by sector and shows each stock's last price and day change.
  3. Press Enter or click a row. The chart, your active studies, and the harmonic patterns reload for that stock.
Your studies stay on when you change stocks. Set up the chart once and then move through the list.

Chart and ranges

  • Range buttons: 6M, 1Y, 2Y (default), 5Y, and All.
  • Scroll to zoom and drag to pan. The legend above the chart shows open, high, low, close, change, and volume for the bar under the cursor.
  • Volume bars are green on up days and red on down days.
  • Prices are split- and dividend-adjusted daily closes.

Changing durations

  • Every study with a period (moving averages, bands, RSI, ATR, and others) shows a row of quick presets under On the chart: 5, 9, 10, 14, 20, 21, 30, 50, 100, 150, 200. Click one, or type any value in its box.
  • + Another adds the same study again with the next classic period (9 → 20 → 50 → 100 → 200). For example, three SMAs at 20, 50, and 200. Each copy has its own line, colour, settings, and Interpretation card.
  • The EMA ribbon has five adjustable lengths (default 8, 13, 21, 34, 55).
  • Picking a study again in its dropdown removes all of its copies. Remove on a card removes only that copy.

Bands, Darvas boxes, and breakouts

  • Any overlay with an upper and a lower line (Bollinger, Keltner, Donchian, envelopes, ATR bands, StdDev channel, Darvas box, breakout range, auto trendlines) is shaded light grey between the lines.
  • Darvas box (Bands and channels): the top is a high not exceeded for 3 sessions; the bottom is a low not undercut for 3 sessions after that. The box is drawn from the day it is confirmed. A close above the top is marked Darvas breakout; a close below the bottom is marked Box broken.
  • Breakout & trend detector (Trend): the prior 20-session range, shaded. A close beyond it on at least 1.5× average volume is marked Breakout or Breakdown. A breakout that closes back inside within 3 sessions is marked Failed breakout. A breakout's trend is followed with a trailing stop (best close minus 3 ATR), drawn as a line, and Trend ended marks where it was hit.
  • Matching strategies in the Breakout group: Darvas box breakout (buy near the 52-week high, stop at the box bottom, raised with each new box) and Volume breakout trend.

Each row in a dropdown is tagged. Overlay studies draw on the price chart. Pane studies open their own panel below the chart. For trend studies, use the Trend dropdown, for example Supertrend, Ichimoku, ADX, or Aroon.

Scott Carney harmonics

Carney all harmonics is on by default and runs on every stock you select. It finds swing highs and lows with a zigzag and checks each run of five pivots (X, A, B, C, D) against Carney's published ratio rules.

Reading the chart

  • The latest 12 patterns are drawn. Solid lines join X-A-B-C-D, and dashed lines join X-B-D and A-C.
  • Green is a bullish pattern, which completes at a low. Red is a bearish pattern, which completes at a high.
  • Each pivot is labelled with its letter. Every completion in the history gets an arrow at D with the pattern name.
  • D is the potential reversal zone (PRZ). It runs to the HOP (harmonic optimal price); a close beyond the HOP fails the pattern.
  • D is only known once price has moved Swing % away from it. Backtests act on that confirmation bar, never on D itself.
  • The legend shows how many patterns are drawn and how many are bullish or bearish.

Picking harmonic studies

The Harmonics dropdown is first under Indicators. Search for a pattern, for example bat or crab, to draw only that pattern. Remove Carney all harmonics if you want only the single pattern.

The Harmonics list button

Click Harmonics list under Analysis to list each drawn pattern with its D date, confirmation date, name, side, PRZ price, HOP, 0.382/0.618 AD targets, and AB, BC, CD, and AD ratios. If the study was removed, the button turns it back on.

Carney PRZ

Carney PRZ (causal trades + live zones) follows Scott Carney's own process: structure, ratio, confirmation. Blue X-A-B-C legs with D and HOP lines show a live setup; PRZ arrows mark past entries, with the exit reason (target, HOP stop, time stop). Confirm 0 enters at the touch of D, 1 on a bullish close above D, 2 on Carney's confirmation. The same rules are the Carney PRZ strategies. On 271 liquid stocks the best causal harmonic variants reach a median return ÷ drawdown of about 0.26, against 1.54 for buy and hold.

Settings

  • Swing % (default 3): the smallest move that counts as a new swing. Lower values find more, smaller patterns. Higher values find fewer, larger ones.
  • Tolerance (default 0.05): how far a ratio can be from the textbook value. Raise it to find more patterns. Lower it for stricter matches.

For one pattern alone, add its own study from the Harmonics section, for example Gartley.

Key levels and pattern breakouts

Two Trend studies follow how the X account @TradZoo writes daily chart posts; TOP computes every level itself.

  • Key levels plan (next session): today's red R2/R1 and green S1/S2 levels, drawn flat across the last 30 sessions. The Interpretation pane writes, for example, "Long above 1,175–1,178, targets 1,185 1,195 1,205+. Below 1,152 the bias turns weak, downside 1,135 1,118 1,098−." R1 and S1 hug the close; R2 and S2 are the next levels out; targets are rounded to about 0.2% steps.
  • Pattern breakout projector: fitted support and resistance lines over 60 bars, shaded grey and named (falling or rising wedge, ascending, descending, or symmetrical triangle, channel, range, broadening). On a close through resistance you get a buying zone, a stop under support, target 1 at the prior swing high, and target 2 at the pattern height projected from the breakout.
  • Strategies: Key level breakout and Pattern breakout (measured move) in the Breakout group.

Interpretation and swing plan

The Interpretation pane under the chart explains in plain English what your selections mean for the current stock. It refreshes whenever you change the stock, add or remove a study, change a setting, pick a strategy, or change capital or cost. Hide collapses it and gives the chart more room.

Left half: swing outlook

  • Headline: the close, the day's change, and the main reasons for the reading: price against the 50- and 200-day averages, RSI, and MACD.
  • Swing bias: Bullish, Neutral, or Bearish, from a simple score of trend, momentum, MACD, the technical rating, and any fresh harmonic pattern.
  • Technical rating: a vote count in the style of TradingView's public technical-ratings method. Twelve SMAs and EMAs, the Ichimoku base line, and a Hull MA each vote buy or sell by where price sits. RSI, Stochastic, CCI, ADX, Awesome, Momentum, MACD, Williams %R, and the Ultimate Oscillator vote buy, sell, or neutral. Hover the badge for the counts.
  • Swing plan: one of these setups:
    SetupWhenTrigger
    Harmonic reversal longA bullish Carney pattern completed in the last 10 sessions and has not failedClose above today's high; stop 1 ATR beyond D; targets 0.382 and 0.618 of the CD leg
    Breakout longBullish, price within half an ATR of the 20-session highClose above that high, ideally on above-average volume; stop 1.5 ATR
    Pullback long in an uptrendBullish and within 2 ATR of the 20-day EMAClose above today's high; stop under the 10-session swing low
    Uptrend, but extendedBullish but more than 2 ATR above the 20-day EMAWait for a pullback toward the EMA
    RangeNeutralBuy near the 20-session low with RSI turning up; exit at the range top
    No long setupBearishStand aside until a close back above the 50-day EMA with RSI over 50. NSE cash shorts are intraday only.
  • Levels: Entry, Stop, T1 (about 1.5R), and T2 (about 3R), where R is the distance from entry to stop. They are drawn on the chart as dashed lines.
  • Steps: the trigger, the stop and its size in ATR, where to take profit and move the stop to break-even, position size for 1% risk of your capital, and a 10–15 session time stop.
  • Same plan on this stock's past: the exact plan replayed on every similar past session of this stock. It shows how often T1 was hit before the stop and the average result in R. A bar that touches both counts as a loss.
  • Base rate: after past sessions in the same trend state with a similar RSI, how often the stock was higher 10 sessions later, plus the median and middle-half range of returns.
The odds and base rate describe what happened on this stock before. They are not a forecast. A result near 50% or near 0R means the setup has had no edge on this name.

Right half: what your selections say

One card per active study and the selected strategy. The item you changed last is highlighted and listed first. Each card gives the current reading in words and a tone (Bullish, Bearish, stretched, or Neutral). What is this? explains the tool. Examples:

  • Moving averages: how far price is from the line, whether the line is rising, and whether a pullback entry is near or price is stretched.
  • Bands and channels: where the close sits in the band and whether the band is in a squeeze.
  • RSI, Stochastic, and other bounded oscillators: overbought or oversold zones, signal-line crosses, and a one-year percentile.
  • MACD and zero-line oscillators: above or below zero, crosses, and whether momentum is building or fading.
  • ADX, Aroon, Ichimoku, EMA ribbon, and Choppiness: whether there is a trend worth following.
  • Volume tools: whether volume confirms the price move or diverges from it.
  • Harmonics: the latest pattern, its ratios, its status (fresh, working, played out, failed, or stale), targets, invalidation, and how past patterns on this stock behaved.
  • Strategy: long or flat today, when it last switched, and how it compared with buy and hold.

YouTube creator strategies

Two sections are named after YouTube channels. Find them in the strategy dropdown and in the indicator dropdowns.

  • @neurotrader888: 9 strategies and 6 studies re-implemented from his published GitHub code. They include directional-change trend, ATR zigzag, auto-trendline breakout, inverse head and shoulders, bull flag/pennant, Hawkes volatility breakout, CMMA vs the Nifty proxy, Donchian with a last-trade filter, and a walk-forward RSI-PCA forecast. Studies: zigzag, auto trendlines, Hawkes volatility, VSA, CMMA, and permutation entropy.
  • @UnbiasedTradingGoshawk: 4 strategies from the public sources his videos test: Connors RSI(2), Connors Double 7s, AHL-style EWMAC trend, and the Lo–Khandani contrarian rule. Studies: EWMAC forecast and return vs the Nifty proxy.

When you pick one, the note under the strategy box shows the creator, a confidence label, source links, and how TOP adapted it. HIGH means the rules come from the creator's own code. MEDIUM means they come from the original public source, because the exact variant in the video is not public. All are long/flat on daily bars and work with Run backtest and Backtest universe.

No YouTube audio or video was downloaded. These are re-implementations of published ideas, not the creators' own results or endorsements. Some rules use an equal-weight Nifty 50 proxy built from the 50 stored stocks as "the market".

Strategy tester

  1. Pick a stock.
  2. Click the Backtest strategy box and type part of a name or idea, for example gartley, breakout, rsi, or reversal. There are 95 strategies, grouped as listed below. The strategy's description appears under the box.
  3. Adjust the strategy's settings if it has any, then set Capital (default ₹10,00,000) and Cost bps (default 5, charged on entry and exit).
  4. Pick Sizing: Full (all equity), Vol target (target vol ÷ the stock's 20-day volatility, capped at 100%), or Half Kelly (half the Kelly fraction from the strategy's earlier trades).
  5. Click Run backtest. Under the result cards, Monte Carlo shows the range of drawdowns from 1,000 reshuffles of the same trades. Walk-forward re-picks the main setting each year from earlier data only and says whether the rule looks over-tuned.

How a backtest runs

  • Each strategy is long or flat. It never shorts.
  • The signal is read at the close and filled at the next session's open. This avoids look-ahead.
  • When long, the full capital is invested.
  • Harmonic strategies go long when a bullish pattern completes and hold for Hold bars (default 10).

Backtest universe

Click Backtest universe (Backtest Nifty 50, ETFs, BSE, or all, following the universe chip) to run the selected strategy, with its current settings, on all 50 Nifty stocks, on all indices, or on the 200 most traded names of a larger universe. The output panel below the chart ranks them by return and shows whether each is long now, its max drawdown, and its trade count. This takes a few seconds. A harmonic strategy takes about 6 seconds.

Strategy leaderboard

Click Leaderboard in the top bar to open a separate page that backtests all 95 strategies, with default settings, on every stock, ETF, and index with at least a year of bars (about 610,000 backtests) and ranks them by CAGR ÷ |max drawdown|.

  • Run backtests starts a background job on all CPU cores at low priority. Progress, speed, and time left update every 15 seconds. Nifty 50 and indices go first, then ETFs, then BSE stocks by traded value. Expect a few hours on a slow PC.
  • Stop and Run / resume pick up where it left off. After a daily sync only scripts with new bars are recomputed. Recompute all starts over.
  • Defaults: CAGR above 15%, at least 3 trades, at least 2 tested years (after the strategy's warm-up), and a median of ₹10 lakh traded a day (indices exempt). Drawdowns under 5% count as 5% in the ratio. Clear Min CAGR to see everything.
  • Filter by universe chip, strategy, or script. Sort by includes Most return ÷ |Max DD| (total return ÷ worst drawdown, 5% floor) next to CAGR ÷ |Max DD|. Tick Best strategy per script to keep each script's top row by that sort; click a column header to sort; Download CSV for every matching row.
  • Strategies that pass most often shows how many scripts each strategy passes on. Click one to filter.
  • Click a row to open that script on the chart with the strategy backtested.
  • Top-N portfolio of one strategy: put the same money (default ₹1 lakh) into each of a strategy's top N stocks (default the #1 strategy and 20 stocks), run the strategy on each, and see the combined money over time with final value, CAGR, max drawdown and CAGR ÷ |max DD| against Nifty 50. The stocks are picked with hindsight, so treat it as a best case.
  • By strategy ranks the strategies themselves by the median of total return ÷ |max drawdown| across every script that passes the filters (no CAGR cut, so losers count; at least 50 scripts). It also shows the middle half, % profitable, % beating buy-and-hold, and the best script. Click a strategy to see its scripts.

Scripts with unreliable data are skipped: traded on fewer than half the weekdays, or a one-day move above +100% or below −50% after split adjustment. Dividend Points and India VIX are left out because they are not prices.

Many strategies on many scripts means some winners are luck. Prefer rules that pass widely, with many trades and full history.

Markets page

Click Markets in the top bar. It shows Nifty's volatility regime (calm, normal or stressed, from its 20-day volatility ranked against the past year) shaded on a Nifty chart; breadth (% of BSE A/B stocks above their 50- and 200-day averages, new highs minus new lows, advances vs declines); sector indices ranked by 3-month return against Nifty with rotation quadrants; daily FII/DII cash flows; and stock scans: Bloodbath, Multi-year breakout, 52-week breakout, Breakout soon, Leaders, Multibaggers, Near 52-week low and Delivery surge. Click a stock to chart it.

Portfolio page

Click Portfolio. Pick a strategy (Momentum 12-1, Low volatility, Sector rotation, ETF momentum), a universe and N, then Run backtest. It ranks the universe at each month-end close and trades at the next open to equal weights, with costs. You get CAGR, drawdown and Sharpe against Nifty, an equity and an underwater chart, current holdings and recent rebalances. Sweep N compares N from 1 to 50. The universe is today's listing, so delisted stocks are missing and results are flattered.

Trend scan

Click Trend scan to run every Trend, Breakout, and Trend reversal rule on the selected stock in one pass. Rules that are long now are listed first, then the rest by return. Each row shows its section, so you can see whether trend-following, breakout, or reversal rules are firing.

Fundamentals

Click Fundamentals to read the public Screener.in company page for the selected stock. It shows:

  • Market cap, current price, high/low, stock P/E, book value, dividend yield, ROCE, ROE, and face value.
  • Screener.in's machine-written pros and cons.
  • A link to the full Screener.in page.

Results are cached for 6 hours, so repeated clicks do not hit Screener.in again. If Screener.in is unreachable or changes its page layout, the panel shows the error.

Verify

Click Verify to run browser-side and server-side checks and list each result as Pass or Fail. They cover:

  • The symbol list, chart canvas, and indicator and strategy menus in the browser.
  • Bars for the selected stock.
  • The indicator and strategy catalogs, the harmonic sections, and the trend reversal rules.
  • A synthetic Gartley match, zigzag pivots, the Screener.in parser, and DuckDB coverage for all 50 names.
  • Every indicator and every strategy backtest on RELIANCE, plus Carney patterns drawn on RELIANCE.

The same server checks run from a terminal:

python -m top.verify

Data and sync

  • Sources: Yahoo Finance daily chart data for the Nifty 50 (adjusted for splits and dividends), the exchanges' daily bhavcopies for BSE stocks and ETFs (adjusted for splits and bonus issues using NSE corporate actions plus a price-gap rule), and the NSE and BSE daily index files for indices (NSE five years; BSE from April 2025).
  • Store: DuckDB file at data/nse.duckdb. It is not in git (about 480 MB), so a new clone starts with the Nifty 50 only.
  • Download ready-made data (shown on the desk while only the Nifty 50 is loaded), or top-sync --snapshot with the server stopped, installs the newest data snapshot from the GitHub releases in a few minutes: every stock, ETF and index, delivery %, and the computed leaderboard. Then use Sync history to add the days since.
  • Sync history refreshes the Nifty 50 and downloads new exchange files (NSE and BSE stocks, ETFs, indices, delivery %) in the background; the first time takes 1–2 hours. The pill shows progress, and the chart reloads when it finishes.
  • The universe follows the current Nifty 50, including M&M, ETERNAL (formerly Zomato), and TMPV (Tata Motors passenger vehicles).

Limits

  • Daily bars only. There is no intraday or live tick data.
  • The swing plan is a rule-based template, not a recommendation. Check liquidity, news, results dates, and your own risk before trading.
  • Backtests are long or flat, with fixed costs and no slippage model, taxes, or position sizing. Past results do not predict future results.
  • Harmonic detection is an approximation of Carney's ratios on a zigzag. It is not certified Harmonic Trader output.
  • Fundamentals are a read of Screener.in's public page. They are not company filings.
  • Charts use TradingView Lightweight Charts (MIT licence). This is not the TradingView platform.
